About

About Muskan Choudhary

I’m pursuing a B.Sc. (Hons.) in Biotechnology at JECRC University in Jaipur. I started with Physics, Chemistry and Biology in school, and I’m now interested in how those subjects come together to explain what happens inside a living cell.

I want to understand how genetic information is passed on, how microbes respond to their surroundings, and how scientists use those processes in biotechnology. The part I find most interesting is working out how you would test an idea and what the results would actually tell you.

As I build practical experience, I want to get comfortable with careful measurement, safe sample handling and clear lab notes. I’d also like to explore how tools such as DNA sequence comparison can help answer questions raised in the lab.

Cell Biology

I’m curious about how a cell keeps so many processes running at once. I want to understand how its compartments divide the work, how its membrane controls what enters and leaves, and how it produces the energy it needs.

Key concepts I want to understand

  • Organelles and their roles
  • Diffusion, osmosis and active transport
  • The cell cycle and mitosis
  • ATP and cellular energy

An example in practice

Osmosis helps explain why a plant cell becomes firm when water enters it.

A question I’d like to explore

How does a cell know when it’s ready to divide?

Genetics

I want to understand how a trait can run in a family and still vary from one person to another. Starting with genes and chromosomes helps me connect the patterns we see in families with what happens when genetic information is passed on.

Key concepts I want to understand

  • DNA, RNA and genes
  • Chromosomes and meiosis
  • Mendelian inheritance
  • Dominant and recessive alleles

An example in practice

A Punnett square can estimate the chances of different offspring genotypes for a simple single-gene cross.

A question I’d like to explore

Why do some traits fit a simple inheritance pattern while others involve many genes?

Human Biology

I’m interested in how body systems adjust together when everyday demands change. Following oxygen from the lungs to working muscles helps me connect breathing, circulation and energy use.

Key concepts I want to understand

  • Circulation and respiration
  • Digestion and nutrient absorption
  • Homeostasis and feedback
  • Innate and adaptive immune defences

An example in practice

During exercise, breathing and heart rate rise as active muscles need more oxygen.

A question I’d like to explore

How does the body sense that its internal conditions are changing?

Microbiology

What interests me about microbes is how much they can change their surroundings despite being so small. I want to understand how bacteria and fungi grow, and how conditions such as temperature and available nutrients affect what they do.

Key concepts I want to understand

  • Bacteria, yeasts and moulds
  • Factors that affect microbial growth
  • Aseptic handling principles
  • Fermentation and food production

An example in practice

In bread dough, yeast ferments sugars and releases carbon dioxide, which helps the dough rise.

A question I’d like to explore

How can changing the conditions favour one microorganism over another?

Plant Biology

I want to understand how a seed becomes a plant that can move water, capture light and keep growing. Looking at roots, stems and leaves together helps me see what each part contributes.

Key concepts I want to understand

  • Plant cells and tissues
  • Water transport through roots and stems
  • Photosynthesis and gas exchange
  • Seed germination and growth

An example in practice

Stomata let carbon dioxide enter leaves for photosynthesis, while water vapour can escape through the same openings.

A question I’d like to explore

How does a plant balance taking in carbon dioxide with conserving water?

Biotechnology in Everyday Life

I’m drawn to the point where something we learn about cells becomes something people can use. I want to understand how biological processes are adapted for food production, healthcare, agriculture and environmental work.

Key concepts I want to understand

  • Vaccines and diagnostic tools
  • Plant biotechnology and crop health
  • Fermentation and industrial enzymes
  • Microbes in environmental cleanup

An example in practice

Some laundry detergents use enzymes to help break down protein or fat stains.

A question I’d like to explore

What makes a biological process reliable enough to use at a larger scale?
